# Coaching

**TL;DR**

Coaching creates structured conversations that translate aspirations into clear experiments, reflection, and renewed commitments. Providing consistent coaching boosts individual confidence, surfaces blockers early, and nurtures a culture of continuous improvement.

## What Is Coaching

Coaching is a focused, goal-oriented relationship between a coach and a coachee who collaborate to strengthen skills, habits, and outcomes. Unlike mentoring, which leans on the mentor’s expertise and long-term advice, a coach emphasizes guided questioning, active listening, and accountability so the coachee can discover their own solutions. Modern workplace coaching can be delivered by trained internal leaders, peer coaches, or external professionals, and sessions often combine empathetic dialogue, feedback, and practical experiments between meetings. When anchored in the team’s strategy, coaching turns abstract development plans into observable behavioural change.

## Why You Might Want Coaching

- **Accelerates capability building.** Regular coaching breaks big growth goals into manageable practices, helping people adopt new skills faster without waiting for annual reviews or classroom training.
- **Raises engagement and retention.** Team members who feel heard and supported are more likely to stay, advocate for the company, and share their own knowledge generously.
- **Improves performance clarity.** Coaches make expectations explicit, align actions with priorities, and provide a neutral channel for surfacing impediments before they turn into crises.
- **Strengthens leadership pipeline.** Training managers to coach expands their toolkit beyond directing and evaluating, reinforcing psychological safety and trust across the organization.
- **Supports change initiatives.** Coaching helps teams experiment with new processes, adapt to reorganizations, and maintain momentum during demanding product or market shifts.

## How to Implement Coaching?

1. **Clarify the purpose and scope.** Define who will receive coaching, the outcomes you expect (for example, faster onboarding or stronger product discovery), and how success will be measured. Secure sponsorship so participants have permission to invest time.
2. **Choose the coaching model.** Decide whether to hire external coaches, train internal leaders, or launch a peer coaching circle. Align availability, confidentiality requirements, and budget with your chosen approach.
3. **Equip coaches and coachees.** Provide practical training on active listening, goal-setting frameworks such as GROW, and how to integrate coaching conversations into regular cadences. Share simple playbooks and reflection templates.
4. **Match pairs thoughtfully.** Consider role, experience, and interpersonal fit. Allow coachees to opt-in or request a different coach if the relationship does not click after the first few sessions.
5. **Set cadence and guardrails.** Agree on session frequency, preparation expectations, and how progress will be captured. Encourage concise action plans between meetings so improvements are visible to both parties.
6. **Inspect and adapt.** Gather feedback at regular intervals, look for leading indicators such as retention or promotion velocity, and adjust the program to keep it relevant as the organization grows.

## Common Pitfalls of Coaching

- **Treating coaching as remedial.** Position coaching as an investment in high-potential talent, not a punishment for poor performers, otherwise people will avoid it or disengage defensively.
- **Inconsistent sponsorship.** Without manager support and protected time, sessions will be cancelled or rushed. Make leaders accountable for enabling the process.
- **Unclear confidentiality boundaries.** Coachees must know what information stays between them and the coach. Agree on reporting expectations upfront to build trust.
- **No follow-through between sessions.** When action plans are not revisited, coaching becomes theoretical. Track commitments visibly and celebrate small wins to reinforce new habits.
- **Over-reliance on external coaches.** Contractors can be valuable, but pairing them with internal champions ensures insights become embedded in the organization instead of walking out the door.
